DicTACtionary

A living glossary of induced proximity science — keeping track of the ever-growing family of TAC degraders and the terminology that surrounds them. Suggest additions any time.

The TAC family & degraders

Modalities that harness induced proximity.

PROTAC

PROteolysis-TArgeting Chimera

The archetypal degrader: a heterobifunctional molecule with one end bound to the target and the other recruiting an E3 ubiquitin ligase, tagging the target for destruction by the proteasome.

Molecular glue (MGD)

Molecular Glue Degrader

A small, monovalent compound that reshapes a protein surface to create or stabilize a protein–protein interface — typically target-to-E3 — driving degradation without a classic two-warhead linker.

LYTAC

LYsosome-TArgeting Chimera

Ferries extracellular and membrane proteins to the lysosome by engaging lysosome-shuttling receptors (e.g. CI-M6PR, ASGPR), reaching targets that the proteasome cannot.

AUTAC

AUtophagy-TArgeting Chimera

A bifunctional degrader that routes targets — including whole organelles such as mitochondria — into the autophagy pathway via a guanine-derived degradation tag.

ATTEC

Autophagosome-TEthering Compound

A small molecule that tethers a target directly to LC3 on the autophagosome membrane, delivering it for autophagic clearance.

AUTOTAC

AUTophagy-TArgeting Chimera

A bifunctional compound that binds the autophagy receptor p62/SQSTM1 to shuttle targets into autophagy.

RIBOTAC

RIBOnuclease-TArgeting Chimera

Extends induced proximity to RNA: a small molecule that binds a structured RNA is linked to a recruiter of endogenous RNase L, enabling selective RNA degradation.

DUBTAC

DeUBiquitinase-TArgeting Chimera

The mirror image of a PROTAC — recruits a deubiquitinase to strip ubiquitin from a target and stabilize it, an example of targeted protein stabilization.

PhosTAC

PHOSphatase-TArgeting Chimera

Induced proximity beyond degradation: recruits a phosphatase to a target to drive its dephosphorylation.

AbTAC

Antibody-based PROTAC

A bispecific antibody that recruits a membrane-bound E3 ligase (e.g. RNF43) to degrade cell-surface proteins.

GlueTAC

Glue-based degrader for surface proteins

A nanobody–covalent-glue conjugate that captures a cell-surface target and routes it to the lysosome for degradation.

TF-PROTAC

Transcription-Factor PROTAC

Couples a DNA oligonucleotide to an E3 ligand, degrading transcription factors by engaging their DNA-binding domains.

PHOTAC / photo-PROTAC

PHOto-activatable degrader

A PROTAC whose degradation activity is switched on by a defined wavelength of light, giving spatiotemporal control over degradation.

dTAG

degradation TAG system

A chemical-genetics tool pairing an FKBP12F36V tag with a selective degrader, enabling acute, reversible depletion of any tagged protein of interest.

HaloPROTAC

HaloTag-directed degrader

Degrades HaloTag-fusion proteins, widely used as a target-validation and proof-of-concept tool.

Induced-proximity concepts

The vocabulary behind the modalities.

Induced proximity

The unifying idea: using a chemical or biologic to force two biomolecules together to drive a programmed outcome — degradation, stabilization, modification, or relocalization.

Targeted Protein Degradation (TPD)

A therapeutic strategy that removes a protein rather than merely inhibiting its activity, expanding the "druggable" proteome to scaffolding and undruggable targets.

Targeted Protein Stabilization (TPS)

The converse of TPD — deliberately increasing a protein's level or half-life, for example with a DUBTAC.

Ternary complex

The three-body assembly (target · degrader · effector, e.g. target–PROTAC–E3) that must form for a bifunctional molecule to act.

Cooperativity (α)

A measure of how much the ternary complex is stabilized (positive cooperativity) or destabilized (negative) relative to the two underlying binary interactions.

Hook effect

Loss of ternary-complex formation at high degrader concentration, as binary complexes outcompete the productive ternary one — producing a characteristic bell-shaped dose–response.

E3 ubiquitin ligase

The enzyme that transfers ubiquitin onto a substrate. Degraders co-opt E3s such as CRBN, VHL, MDM2, and the IAPs to mark new targets.

CRBN & VHL

Cereblon and von Hippel–Lindau — the two most widely used E3 recruiters in degrader design, each with well-characterized small-molecule ligands.

Neo-substrate

A protein that is not a natural client of an E3 ligase but is recruited and degraded thanks to a molecular glue or PROTAC.

Event-driven pharmacology

Activity that depends on a transient event (such as ubiquitin transfer) rather than sustained target occupancy — the basis for catalytic, sub-stoichiometric action.

Catalytic / sub-stoichiometric

A single degrader molecule can drive many rounds of target degradation, in contrast to occupancy-driven inhibitors that act one-to-one.

Linkerology

The art of tuning the chemical tether that joins the two warheads of a bifunctional degrader; linker length and composition strongly shape ternary complex formation and potency.

Degron

A sequence or chemical mark that directs a protein toward the degradation machinery.

DC50 / Dmax

Core potency metrics for degraders — the concentration giving 50% degradation, and the maximum extent of degradation achieved.
